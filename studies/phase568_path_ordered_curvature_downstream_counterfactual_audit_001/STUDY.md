# Phase 568 — path-ordered-curvature downstream counterfactual audit

This deterministic, zero-sampling phase compares the frozen registered
complete-lattice action with the prospective study-local curvature ordering
selected by Phase567. The Phase567 study-local candidate source is compile-
linked and exact-bound; Phase568 does not carry a second selectable candidate
implementation. It does not edit or replace the registered operator.

The implementation first validates the candidate curvature, its differential,
its transpose, and its action gradient.  It then requires the candidate and
registered constructions to agree at the origin, where their common linear
coboundary makes their Hessians identical.  Only after those controls pass does
it compare actions, gradients, homogeneous components, and full Hessian
spectra at the six exact-bound Phase548 checkpoint positions used by Phase550.

The old spectra are reused from the exact-bound Phase550 artifact.  Candidate
dense forms are built one at a time under a pre-allocation resource refusal.
All spectral comparisons use a shared roundoff floor derived from both
branches.  A favorable terminal is workbench evidence only: the candidate is
not source-selected, the Phase548 convergence failure is not reinterpreted,
and no sampling or downstream authority follows.

The first v1 execution was deliberately stopped after its first checkpoint row
when adversarial review found that governance and resource assertions were not
fully machine-checked. It emitted no terminal output. The v1 contract remains
immutable and the partial observation is preserved under `output/attempts/`.
The v2 contract strengthens those integrity checks without changing the
candidate, numerical menu, thresholds, or scientific terminal taxonomy.
Adversarial pre-execution review then required v3 to bind the finalized program
and the complete v1/v2 predecessor chain, and to machine-assert every declared
control and spectral/resource governance flag. V3 again changes no scientific
formula, menu, tolerance, or terminal.
