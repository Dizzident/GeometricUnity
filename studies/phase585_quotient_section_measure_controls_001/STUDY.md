## Section and three different actions

Write d0 e=de+A0 e-e A0, C_e=e^-1 d0 e, A=A0+c p,
B=A0+C_e, T=c p-C_e and

    Phi=e T e^-1=c Ad(e)p-(d0 e)e^-1.

For the right tilted action e'=eh, p'=Ad(h^-1)p+c C_h, the ordinary
product rule gives C_eh=Ad(h^-1)C_e+C_h. Hence Phi'=Phi. If the allowed
gauge group includes e^-1, choose h=e^-1: e'=I, p'=c Phi, B'=A0 and
A'=A0+Phi. The inverse ordinary derivative is
d(e^-1)=-e^-1(de)e^-1, so the derivative of e e^-1 is also zero.
The section is unique: eh=I forces h=e^-1, including its derivatives as a
field. The action on the full pair is free even when A0 has stabilizers.
The map to (A,B) may discard stabilizer information, so its quotient need
not be the quotient of the full pair. Boundary conditions, allowed components,
bundle topology and the existence of a global e can obstruct this section;
this pointwise calculation does not settle those conditions.

A change of background frame k also transforms
A0'=k A0 k^-1-(dk)k^-1, e'=k e k^-1 and p'=k p k^-1, with ordinary
derivatives transformed by the product rule. Then Phi'=k Phi k^-1.
In contrast, left multiplication by the seed (k,0) sends e to ke and leaves
p and A0 fixed; it descends to

    Phi' = k Phi k^-1-(d0 k)k^-1.

Thus A0+Phi has a left connection law. This algebraic left action need not
be a symmetry of a proposed action. The right redundancy, background frame
change and left seed transformation are distinct. The exact battery checks
all three laws with nonzero A0 and nonconstant first jets.

## What the Jacobian does and does not give

Let a finite retained real coefficient space V=R^D be preserved by a compact
adjoint representation R(e), with its invariant Euclidean metric. At fixed
epsilon data, Phi=c R(e)p+b(e). In any local chart u of those data,

    D(Phi,u)/D(p,u) = [ c R(e)   partial_u Phi ]
                       [    0          I       ].

Its absolute determinant is one. An unchanged Haar chart density, or another
unchanged density in u, multiplies both sides and cancels. Thus unrestricted
coefficient Lebesgue measure is preserved conditionally. Any cutoff domain must
be transported with the affine map. A cutoff held fixed without invariance is
a different integral. If the retained subspace is not closed under R(e), a
projected map can be singular and the unit-Jacobian argument does not apply.

The compact SO(3) toy below has three coefficient and three group coordinates.
Its full 6x6 Jacobian is evaluated by independent Leibniz permutation sum,
pivoted elimination and central differences of an actual local rotation chart.
The first-jet coordinate map also has three independent derivative coordinates;
its full 9x9 Jacobian is evaluated, retaining the nonzero off-diagonal blocks.
The latter uses ordinary coordinate/Lebesgue densities, not compact Haar for
all nine coordinates. SO(3) first jets have noncompact R^3 derivative fibers
per derivative direction; there is no normalized Haar probability on that
full group. Gauge volume cannot be silently integrated out to one there.
Neither finite result is a continuum functional determinant.

For a compact toy with a descending integrand, normalized compact Haar can

## Compact toy, its limitation, and a failed graph derivative

The toy sets C_h=Ad(h^-1)B-B for a fixed Lie vector B=(1,0,0); equivalently
d_B h=[B,h] with no spatial derivative. It is an exact compact cocycle and
gives Phi=c R(e)p+B-R(e)B. It is only an affine algebra control, not an
exact representation of arbitrary spatial source jets. The 24 signed proper
rotations provide an exact finite subgroup census, not a quadrature claiming
to integrate every function over SO(3).

There is a precise conditional limitation: for a continuous right cocycle
C_gh=R(h)^-1 C_g+C_h of a compact group into a finite real representation,
normalized Haar averaging over g gives m=R(h)^-1 m+C_h, hence
C_h=m-R(h)^-1 m. Every such cocycle is a coboundary. Our toy has m=-B;
the finite subgroup also averages to -B exactly. A group value alone cannot
encode arbitrary continuum derivative fibers: at h=I an arbitrary first jet
can have dh nonzero, while a group cocycle has C_I=0. This excludes neither
nonlinear group-valued link regulators, continuum jets, nor fixing the epsilon
section before discretizing Phi. It is not a no-go for all finite regulators.

and let Df=f1-f0. Then
